# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
Independent developers, open source maintainers, and technical audiences who want to understand a codebase at a glance. They arrive with a local Git repository and want an immediate visual story of how the project has grown, contracted, and changed over time.

## Product Purpose
Stoke Your Code turns Git history into a K-line narrative. Instead of reading commits or dashboards, users should instantly see the rhythm of a project through daily candles, churn, and momentum. Success means the landing page explains the concept in seconds and makes the product feel inevitable.

## Brand Personality
Precise, sharp, cinematic. The product should feel like a trading terminal for software history, with the clarity of a good charting tool and the intrigue of a strong visual artifact.

## Anti-references
Avoid generic SaaS hero layouts, soft pastel startup aesthetics, bloated card grids, and anything that looks like a settings dashboard. Do not make this feel like a tutorial site or an analytics admin panel. The page should not depend on user interaction to communicate the idea.

## Design Principles
1. Show the concept before explaining it.
2. Let the K-line chart carry the narrative weight.
3. Make software history feel legible, not technical.
4. Favor terminal-grade precision over marketing fluff.
5. Keep the page low-interaction and high-comprehension.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Target WCAG AA contrast. Preserve legibility on desktop and mobile. Motion should be restrained and non-essential. Color cannot be the only way to distinguish up/down states; shape, labels, and context should support interpretation.
